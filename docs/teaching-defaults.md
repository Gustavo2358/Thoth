# General teaching defaults

These principles are product policy, not learner-specific findings. A TeachingDirective requires learner evidence. Research motivates the policy; it does not establish the best method for this person or an expected effect size for ChatGPT Voice.

| Default in the teacher prompt | Practical behavior | Research context |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval before model | Let the learner attempt before supplying wording when appropriate. | Retrieval practice can strengthen later retention; transferring that result to an oral AI conversation is an application, not a measured result here. |
| Selective corrective feedback followed by production | Respond to meaning, focus on a valuable issue, then invite another attempt. | Oral corrective feedback has evidence of benefit in classroom second-language learning; forms, contexts and learners differ. |
| Task repetition with variation | Reuse the same communicative capability later in another situation. | Repeated speech can support fluency; varying context also makes transfer more informative than literal echo. |
| Distributed revisiting | Return to a few relevant earlier capabilities over sessions. | Distributed practice benefits retention in many learning tasks; this product's scheduling heuristics are not an experimentally optimized spacing schedule. |
| Supported performance differs from transfer | Preserve support metadata; test later unassisted use without revealing wording. | A successful immediate reproduction and later unaided retrieval provide different evidence. No numerical mastery inference is justified. |

Selected background references:

- Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning*, Science. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408).
- Lyster & Saito (2010), *Oral Feedback in Classroom SLA: A Meta-Analysis*, Studies in Second Language Acquisition. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520).
- de Jong & Perfetti (2011), *Fluency Training in the ESL Classroom: An Experimental Study of Fluency Development and Proceduralization*, Language Learning. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00620.x).
- Cepeda et al. (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis*, Psychological Bulletin. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354).

Conversation quality remains a constraint: accept valid alternatives, follow interesting tangents, avoid overcorrection, do not reveal private targets, and let goal-led communication take precedence over completing learning intentions. External teacher behavior and actual learning gains need observation in the personal pilot.
